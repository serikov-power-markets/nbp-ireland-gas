# From the NBP to the island of Ireland: gas flows, the link to the I-SEM, a capacity booking exercise and a reporting check

Research exercise on open data. What does the gas chain that feeds Ireland look like in numbers (British System Average Price, physical flow at Moffat), how much of that flow is the power sector, what do gas-fired plants earn against the day-ahead price, how should exit capacity be booked when a daily forecast is available, and can a trade file be checked against a reporting schema?

**What is real and what is assumed.** Real: National Gas Transmission REST data (daily physical flow at Moffat 22 Nov 2021 to 21 Nov 2026, prevailing nominations at Moffat), ONS series of the daily System Average Price (SAP) of gas (National Gas data, 2018 to Nov 2026), ECB EUR/GBP daily rates, and from the sibling project `ie-isem-market` the ENTSO-E I-SEM day-ahead prices, the gas-fired generation and the EUA price. **Assumed** (parameters in the code, not read from a source): energy content of gas (11.0 GWh per mscm), power plant efficiency 0.5 and CO2 factor 0.202 t per MWh gas; the overrun charge (a multiple of the daily price, varied 1.5 to 6, because the GNI Code of Operations was not read); the 2025/26 tariff applied to every test year. **Read from sources on 23 Nov 2026:** the GNI transmission tariffs for gas year 2025/26 (Moffat Entry annual 436.79 EUR per peak day MWh, short-term multipliers; `data/raw/gni_tariffs_2025_26/`) and the field list of Table 1 of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 1348/2014 (`data/raw/remit/`). The first booking exercise below (`booking.py`) still uses assumed relative prices and is kept for comparison. The NBP price itself is not open: SAP (the daily average of balancing-market trades) is used as the British gas price. The Moffat flow is gas leaving the British system towards the whole island (Northern Ireland, the Republic and the Isle of Man), not the Irish Republic alone. The EUA series ends in 2025, so the 2026 clean spark spread uses the last value carried forward.

## Results (`results/summary.json`)
- **The chain in numbers.** Moffat flow 57 to 63 TWh a year (2022 60.5, 2023 57.4, 2024 63.3, 2025 63.2), mean 169 GWh a day, peak 339 GWh on 8 January 2025; winter days (Dec to Feb) carry 1.37 times the summer flow, and the 95th percentile day is 1.48 times the mean.
- **Power sector share.** Daily Moffat flow regressed on the gas burned by Irish power plants (generation divided by 0.5) with month effects, 1,398 days since 2023: R2 0.715 against 0.372 for month effects alone; 1 GWh of gas burned in power plants goes with 1.35 GWh more flow (t 18.9, Newey-West errors, 7 lags), i.e. more than one for one because the power sector is the swing demand and heating moves with it on the same cold days. Mean burn for power 55 GWh a day against 168 GWh at Moffat, about a third. The correlation of the daily flow with the I-SEM price is 0.51.
- **Spark spread.** Fuel-only spark spread (I-SEM price minus SAP in EUR divided by 0.5) is 42 to 44 EUR/MWh in 2023 to 2026; with carbon it falls to 9.8 (2023), 15.3 (2024), 14.1 (2025) and 9.4 (2026, EUA carried forward). SAP averaged 39.5, 33.9, 35.6 and 52.8 EUR/MWh in those years (ONS data, EUR from the ECB rate).
- **Capacity booking exercise (`booking_by_gas_year.csv`).** Exit capacity at Moffat is booked as an annual base plus a daily top-up to a plain forecast (same weekday last week), with an overrun charge. The base is chosen on the previous gas year and tested on the next one (three gas years 2024 to 2026): the trained base costs 0.1% above the best base chosen with hindsight on average (0.0, 0.3, 0.0%), booking last year's peak 27.9% above (16.8 to 38.7%), booking the 95th percentile 7.3% above (1.7 to 11.5%). The trained base is about 60% of the peak day (203 to 215 GWh a day against 299 to 339). A perfect forecast would lower the cost by about 9% (78.6 against 86.3 thousand units in 2024). All of this rests on the assumed price ratios; with different ratios the best base moves, the method does not.
- **Capacity booking with the published GNI Moffat Entry tariff (`gni_booking.py`, `booking_gni_tariff.csv`).** Annual capacity costs 436.79 EUR per peak day MWh for the gas year; a day in July costs 0.21 EUR per MWh (0.0484% of the annual price) and a day in January 8.70, so summer days are cheaper to book daily than to hold annually (an annual booking costs 1.20 EUR per MWh and day). A base is chosen on the previous gas year and tested on the next (2024 to 2026, flow at Moffat treated as if one shipper booked all of it): the base trained on the previous year costs 0.7% above the hindsight base at an overrun multiple of 3 (1.2% at 1.5, 1.9% at 6; 0.1 to 3.6% in single runs), booking last year's peak 9.5 to 19.4% above on average (1 to 31% in single runs), last year's 95th percentile 1.5 to 3.8% above on average (0 to 10%); adding a monthly top-up (previous year's monthly 90th percentile) is within -3.4 to +4.8% of hindsight and not clearly better. The trained base is 219 to 286 GWh a day (the peak day is 299 to 339). With perfect forecasts the cost is 1 to 14% lower than with the naive forecast. The overrun multiple is an assumption, so the ranking of strategies is read, not the euro totals (112 to 135 million EUR a year for the whole flow at the hindsight optimum).
- **Reporting check (`remit.py`).** A working schema (not the official one) and a validator; a synthetic file of 40 trades with 7 injected defects (negative price, short LEI, delivery end not after start, duplicate UTI, counterparty equal to reporting party, wrong buy/sell code, unknown currency): all 7 found, plus the second row of the duplicate pair, and no findings on the clean file.

- **Reporting check on the official Table 1 (`remit_table1.py`).** The 58 fields of Table 1 of Regulation 1348/2014 as a registry, a validator that implements only the rules the regulation text states (code types of fields 2, 5, 7 and 9, action types of field 58, voice-brokered Y or blank, option style and type, settlement and load type values, termination date blank when equal to the delivery end, counterparty different from the reporting party, delivery end not before start, reporting by the end of the next working day under Article 7(1)) and a synthetic file of 40 reports with 9 injected defects: all 9 found (plus the second row of the duplicate pair), nothing else flagged, clean file without findings.

## What this does not show
No trade execution, no nominations or capacity bookings were made; the tariff is the GNI Moffat Entry tariff of one gas year (2025/26) applied to earlier years, other GNI and National Gas products and the GB UNC are not modelled; no GNI transparency data (Gas Networks Ireland transparency data were not used, so nothing about the Irish side of the chain beyond Moffat); the Moffat entry capacity auction series returned no data through the API and is not used; the booking study has three test years and a naive forecast; the NBP-to-Ireland spread is not measured (no NBP price, no Irish price). It is not a statement about the GNI Code of Operations or the GB UNC, which I have not read. The first reporting schema (`remit.py`) is a working schema; `remit_table1.py` follows the field list of Table 1 but not the formats, code lists and mandatory flags of the ACER user manual and the XSD schemas, and not the 2026 amendment of the regulation (not read); nothing was submitted to any repository and no EMIR report is covered.
